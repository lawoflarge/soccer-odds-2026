How the group stage worked

Forty-eight teams were split into twelve groups of four. Each team played the other three in its group once, so every group produced six matches and every team played three.

The qualification rule is the part that surprises people who remember earlier tournaments. The top two of each group advanced, which is twenty-four teams, and then the eight best third-placed teams were added on top. That is thirty-two survivors out of forty-eight, so two thirds of the field came through. A third place was often enough, which changed how much a single bad result actually cost.

Ranking inside a group went by points first, then goal difference, then goals scored. That is also the order the tournament simulation uses when it plays the draw out ten thousand times, and it is why goal difference carries real weight in a three match group: with so few matches, one heavy win can outrank a whole extra point.

What the percentages mean

Each row shows the fair chance of a home win, a draw and an away win, taken from the odds several bookmakers published and cleaned of the bookmaker margin. They are not predictions of a single result and they are not advice to place a bet. The method is set out in turning odds into probability.

The last two columns are goal markets. Over 2.5 is the chance the match finished with three or more goals in total, BTTS is the chance both teams scored at least once. Both are explained in goal markets explained.
